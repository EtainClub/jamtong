There was once a boy, born in a slash-and-burn farming village deep in the backcountry where there was no electricity and no cars, who grew up like a wild marten with the fireflies that lit the dark as his companions. The boy, who despite his poverty walked fifteen li of mountain paths to elementary school, came up after graduating to a rented room on a Seongnam hillside and, at the tender age of thirteen, began life as a boy factory worker. While others his age went to school in uniforms, he had to stand before rough, dangerous machines, and amid constant beatings and brutal labor he suffered more than a hundred wounds, large and small, all over his body. When a press crushed his wrist, the growth plate of the bone stopped and his arm became badly twisted, shattering even his humble dream of becoming a skilled technician and giving his mother an easier life.

In the despair of becoming disabled and finding it hard to go on even as a laborer, unable to attend school or cram school, the seventeen-year-old boy twice tried to give up on life. But having miraculously survived thanks to a pharmacist who sensed what he intended and handed him digestive medicine instead, the boy changed his mind and chose to hold on to a thread of hope rather than despair. He wrote three goals in his diary, to live without being beaten by others, to escape poverty, and to travel the world freely, and threw himself into learning. In a cramped rented room with no space even for a desk, on nights when his family was asleep, he turned an outdoor water barrel upside down in the yard and turned the pages of his books by the light that leaked from a neighbor's window. After passing the high-school and university entrance equivalency exams this way, he had only eight months to get into university.

While working at a factory to earn his cram school fees, he was beaten by a senior worker for reading books and suffered a broken rib, yet he never gave up. Swallowing the pain, he worked by day and ran to cram school at night, and until the four o'clock curfew lifted at dawn he wrestled with blood-stained study guides, pressing thumbtacks into his desk to keep himself awake. The boy worker who had started out below 300,000th in the mock exams achieved the miracle of reaching around 2,000th nationwide on the entrance exam in eight months, and entered Chung-Ang University's law school as a specially treated scholarship student with full tuition and a living allowance. With a scholarship three times what he had earned at the factory, he even supported his older brother's studies for the university entrance exam.

After entering university, he came face to face on video with the horrors of the Gwangju Democratization Movement, which he had known only through the press, and he realized the reality of the military dictatorship, feeling deep shock and shame. He could not join the protests right away, but he left his friends a promise: if he later passed the bar exam, instead of becoming a judge or prosecutor he would become a human rights lawyer and be a light for the weak in the shadows. After passing the bar, he turned down the guaranteed path of success, wealth and glory, returned to Seongnam where he had lived as a boy worker, and worked as a human rights lawyer who defended people, not money, and justice, not profit, becoming a steadfast companion to powerless neighbors.

When the large hospitals in Seongnam's old downtown closed and the emergency medical system collapsed, he gathered the will of 200,000 citizens and drafted a citizens' initiative ordinance to establish a municipal hospital. But after witnessing the local council scrap the citizens' earnest wish in just forty-seven seconds, he ran for mayor of Seongnam in order to change, himself, a politics that treated citizens like dogs and pigs. After becoming mayor he finally completed the Seongnam municipal medical center, recorded a high rate of keeping his pledges, and moved on to become governor of Gyeonggi Province, raising residents' pride. He then ran for president, but lost by a narrow margin of 0.73 percentage points; bowing his head in apology to the supporters who had given their all through the night, he conceded cleanly and unconditionally to prevent division.

According to the author, after the new government took office he faced a fierce judicial assault, with an investigative team the size of a district prosecutors' office and more than five hundred search-and-seizure raids, but they could not manufacture crimes that did not exist. In January 2024, as leader of the opposition party, he stood at the crossroads of life and death when an assailant stabbed him in the neck with a knife, but having miraculously survived, he came to regard the rest of his life as a bonus and became calmer and stronger still. Then, during the illegal declaration of emergency martial law on December 3 of that year, he climbed over the wall of the National Assembly at the risk of his life, helped bring about the lifting of martial law, and, together with the sovereign people, defended democracy and opened the door to a new era. The more than a hundred wounds left on the boy worker's body and all of life's hardships stand as an undying record of a fierce fight to protect not private interest but the public good and justice, and as the mark of a great journey taken by one human being who let light in through his wounds and set out to become a steadfast light for the weak.
